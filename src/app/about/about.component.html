What Does Silloker Do?

The Silloker seeks to use computational methods to open creative avenues into studying Korea’s last kingdom, the Chosŏn dynasty (1392-1910). The title of this project originates in a textual compilation titled Sillok (“veritable records”), which comprise daily accounts of dynastic affairs for almost five centuries. This is a staggering 49,646,667 characters and 1,894 books on topics as varied as diplomacy, economy, religion, daily court life, and natural phenomena – a rare feat in the history of book-keeping that in 1997, gained rightful registration as UNESCO’s intangible heritage. Within a decade thereafter, the sillok was transcribed and translated (from literary Chinese to Korean) and digitized in entirety; this made an unprecedented memory of the past available on a public web domain (http://sillok.history.go.kr). This collection carries so much potential to provide significant insights into not only Korean history, but also the neighboring nations, religion, geography, climate, and so on. The Sillok Project will attempt to bridge the gap between these essential unanswered questions and the historians that are often limited by the lack of appropriate tools or technical knowledge. The project will create an online service for scholars to effectively perform complicated searches by crawling on the sillok server, then parsing, tagging and storing important attributes to the millions of simple text entries onto my own database.

Two major features of Silloker are:
1) Provide shape and visualization of the data on various dimensions.
2) Search and download silloker entries that correspond to search entries.

Record Types Offered:
  • Wangjo Sillok (왕조실록)
  • Secretariat (승정원일기)
  • Garrison Record (군영등록)
